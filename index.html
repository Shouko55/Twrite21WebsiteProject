5 Note-Taking Apps That Actually Work

By the StudyHub Team · Published May 12, 2025 · 4 min read

We tested five popular note-taking apps for one month, using each one for real class notes. Here's what we found ranked from best to worst for college students on a budget.

1. Notion (Free for students)

Best for: All-in-one workspace notes, to-do lists, and databases in one place.

Pros: Extremely flexible, free with a student email, syncs across devices.
Cons: Has a learning curve can feel overwhelming at first.

2. Obsidian (Free)

Best for: Connecting ideas with linked notes great for research and long-term knowledge.

Pros: Works offline, stores files locally, powerful linking.
Cons: No built-in sync unless you pay, less, beginner-friendly.

3. Microsoft OneNote (Free)

Best for: Handwritten notes on tablets and laptops with stylus support.

Pros: Excellent free-form canvas, free with a Microsoft account.
Cons: Sync can be slow organization can get messy.

4. Google Keep (Free)

Best for: Quick, short notes and reminders.

Pros: Fast, simple, syncs with Google account.
Cons: No advanced formatting limited for long notes.

5. Evernote (Free)

Best for: Web clipping and scanning paper documents.

Pros: Powerful search, OCR for scanned notes.
Cons: Free tier is now very limited best features require payment.

Quick Comparison

App Price Best For Offline?
Notion Free (students) All-in-one Limited
Obsidian Free Linked notes Yes
OneNote Free Handwriting Yes
Google Keep Free Quick notes Limited
Evernote Free Web clipping Yes

The Pomodoro Method, Explained

By the StudyHub Team · Published May 8, 2025 · 3 min read

The Pomodoro Technique is one of the simplest and most effective time-management methods for studying. It was developed by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s and is still used by millions of students today.

How It Works

The method is simple: work in focused 25-minute blocks, then take a 5-minute break. After four blocks, take a longer break of 15–30 minutes.

  • Step 1: Choose a task.
  • Step 2: Set a timer for 25 minutes.
  • Step 3: Work until the timer rings. No distractions.
  • Step 4: Take a 5-minute break.
  • Step 5: After four Pomodoros, take a 15–30 minute break.

Why It Works

Studies on attention and fatigue show that the human brain can only sustain deep focus for limited periods. Short, timed work sessions match the brain's natural attention cycle and prevent burnout.

"The Pomodoro Technique helps you focus on one task at a time and eliminates the anxiety of a large, overwhelming project." — Francesco Cirillo

Tips for Students

  • Turn off notifications during your 25-minute block.
  • Keep a "distraction list" — write down anything you want to check later.
  • Use the break to stand up, stretch, or drink water — not to scroll social media.

Cirillo, F. (2025). The Pomodoro Technique. https://francescocirillo.com/pages/pomodoro-technique

How to Cite Sources (APA & MLA)

By the StudyHub Team · Published May 3, 2025 · 5 min read

Two citation styles dominate college writing: APA (social sciences) and MLA (humanities). Here's a quick cheat sheet for the most common sources.

APA 7th Edition — Quick Reference

Book:

Author, A. A. (Year). Title of work. Publisher.

Example:

Brown, P. C. (2014). Make it stick. Harvard University Press.

Website article:

Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day). Title of article. Site Name. URL

Example:

StudyHub Team. (2025, May 12). 5 note-taking apps that actually work. StudyHub. https://studyhub.example/apps

MLA 9th Edition — Quick Reference

Book:

Author Last, First. Title of Work. Publisher, Year.

Example:

Brown, Peter C. Make It Stick. Harvard UP, 2014.

Website article:

Author Last, First. "Title of Article." Site Name, Date, URL.

Example:

StudyHub Team. "5 Note-Taking Apps That Actually Work." StudyHub, 12 May 2025, studyhub.example/apps.

Side-by-Side Comparison

Feature APA 7 MLA 9
Used in Social sciences Humanities
Author name format Last, F. M. Last, First
In-text citation (Author, Year) (Author Page)
Reference list title "References" "Works Cited"

Best Free Productivity Tools for Students

By the StudyHub Team · Published April 28, 2025 · 4 min read

You don't need expensive software to stay organized. These three tools are completely free for students and cover the essentials: task management, scheduling, and note organization.

1. Trello (Free)

Best for: Visual task management using boards and cards.

Create a board for each class. Add cards for assignments and drag them across columns: "To Do," "In Progress," and "Done." The free plan allows unlimited boards, cards, and lists.

2. Google Calendar (Free)

Best for: Scheduling classes, deadlines, and study blocks.

Use color-coding for different subjects. Set reminders 1 day and 1 hour before deadlines. Sync it with your phone so you never miss a due date.

3. Notion (Free for students)

Best for: Combining notes, tasks, and databases in one workspace.

Notion's free student plan includes unlimited pages and blocks. Use it to build a personal dashboard with links to your notes, calendar, and to-do list.

Trello Visual task boards Yes (unlimited) Yes
Google Calendar Scheduling Yes Yes
